\section*{Supplementary Note S1. Evaluation Environment and Data Identifiers}
\noindent\textbf{Chat wrapping and stopping.} Each benchmark prompt below was passed as the content of one user message to the Qwen tokenizer's
\texttt{apply\_chat\_template} method with \texttt{add\_generation\_prompt=True}. Greedy evaluation used
vLLM with bfloat16 weights, seed 0, temperature 0, an 8,192-token generation limit, a 10,240-token context,
and the model's default EOS stopping behavior. Pass@$k$ used the same wrapping with seed 0, temperature
0.6, top-$p=0.95$, and a 4,096-token cap. All training runs and the base model used Qwen model snapshot
\texttt{989aa7980e4cf806f80c7fef2b1adb7bc71aa306}.

\noindent\textbf{Hardware and software.} The base model and all 30 step-300 checkpoints were evaluated on NVIDIA H100 NVL GPUs (driver 575.57.08) with
vLLM 0.10.0, PyTorch 2.7.1+cu128, and Transformers 4.53.2, using one packaged set of frozen prompt files and
one model per GPU. Generation ran on the cluster; scoring ran on the original workstation with the unchanged
scorers. Every returned output file is checked against the SHA-256 recorded when it was generated. Countdown prompts were generated for the base model and all checkpoints on one two-RTX-4090 workstation with the same vLLM, PyTorch, and Transformers versions. The base model and all 30 checkpoints were sampled on one workstation with four RTX A6000 GPUs and vLLM 0.10.0; outputs were scored on the original machine with the unchanged scorer. Re-sampling the base model on the same hardware keeps the hardware and software common within each model--base contrast. Sampled pass@1 is an average over stochastic outputs, not the greedy accuracy reported in the main benchmark tables.

\noindent\textbf{Checkpoint identifiers.} The manifest \path{eval/CHECKPOINT_MANIFEST.json} identifies the 30 step-300 checkpoints by per-file and aggregate SHA-256 hashes; the evaluation package verifies every file against these hashes before generation.

\noindent\textbf{Dataset identifiers.} GSM8K used the 1,319-item test parquet distributed with the local Verl data preparation. Its upstream revision
was not retained, but the exact evaluated file is vendored as \path{eval/data/gsm8k_test.parquet} with SHA-256
\path{f284e0ad015991d0059d265051a3c0915c5c1def15a8c01ac9a9142eeb87a180}. Exact comparison of every saved
input/reference row with locally cached immutable snapshots recovered the other revisions: MATH used all seven
test configurations from \texttt{EleutherAI/hendrycks\_math} at
\path{21a5633873b6a120296cce3e2df9d5550074f4a3} (5,000/5,000 rows); BBH used all 27 test configurations from
\texttt{SaylorTwift/bbh} at \path{b5306be6f827cfafbb545ff5a51f96916029b0fd} (6,511/6,511 rows); and MMLU-Pro
used the test split of \texttt{TIGER-Lab/MMLU-Pro} at
\path{dd36ce4b34827164989f100331f82c5a29741747} (12,032/12,032 rows). The MATH-500 pass@$k$ subset is an exact
500-item subset of that pinned MATH snapshot and is preserved as \path{eval/data/math500.jsonl}, SHA-256
\path{200806fb17234213e909649c09a14ce01ef836b32d3bcb1a261fcf96cda4470f}. The machine-readable manifest
\path{eval/DATASET_MANIFEST.json} records canonical input digests, and \path{eval/verify_dataset_manifest.py}
checks the archived files offline and can reconstruct the three revision-pinned datasets.

\noindent\textbf{Archived code and outputs.} The prompt builders are archived in \path{eval/eval_gsm8k_prompt_ablation.py} and
\path{eval/eval_gsm8k_prompt_open_tag_ablation.py}. The variants were generated with the same package,
hardware, decoding, and 8,192-token limit as the primary prompts, for the base model and all 30 checkpoints. The saved per-item files contain the raw response, extracted strict and flexible predictions, correctness flags, and finish reason so that extraction can be re-audited without regenerating model responses.

\section*{Supplementary Note S2. Additional Prompt Diagnostics}
For the base model, removing the opening reasoning tag recovered 14.9 percentage points relative to the original wrapper; removing the complete prefill recovered 19.3 points. The latter condition differed from the standard prompt by 1.9 points; this difference cannot be attributed to the remaining prompt differences, because the paired exact McNemar p-value is 0.14, although this is not evidence that the two prompts are equivalent.

Early-short checkpoints failed under the think prompt in two different ways. The 15 early-short GRPO and DAPO-style checkpoints closed the reasoning block almost immediately (median response 13--250 tokens; at most 1.7\% of items reached the 8,192-token limit). The three early-short PPO checkpoints instead produced unbounded output: 96.5--100\% of items reached the limit, so flexible scoring credited almost none. PPO seed 10 also reached the limit on 34.1\% of standard-prompt items, by repeating one word or a short phrase until the limit, which accounts for its low standard-prompt accuracy. Prompt changes interacted with these modes: the clean tag instruction reduced limit-reaching to 8.0\% for PPO seed 10 and 61.3\% for PPO seed 8, and removing the complete prefill reduced it from 68.2\% to 1.3\% for PPO seed 14, a run just above the early-short threshold.

\noindent\textbf{Countdown without the training wrapper.} We also posed the 1,024 Countdown test items as a plain request, without role lines, prefill, or tags, asking for the final equation on a last line \texttt{Answer: <equation>} (prompt in Appendix A of the article), with greedy decoding. Checkpoints that did not shorten early averaged 49.9\% under the training wrapper and 44.4\% under the plain prompt (9 of 12 significantly lower, 2 higher; exact McNemar tests), and early-short checkpoints averaged 26.2\% and 22.6\% (9 of 18 lower, 1 higher). Under the plain prompt, checkpoints that did not shorten early gave a final answer on a median 63.8\% of items, versus 98.2\% under the wrapper. The two largest gains came from explosion checkpoints: PPO seeds 14 and 8 scored 0.0\% under the wrapper, where they produced 8,192 tokens of degenerate text, but 44.1\% and 21.1\% under the plain prompt, so their degeneration was specific to the training wrapper. GRPO seed 8, by contrast, answered every plain-prompt item with ``Okay.'' The base model scored 11.2\% under the wrapper and 1.8\% under the plain prompt.

\section*{Supplementary Note S3. Additional Training Diagnostics}
\noindent\textbf{Clip fractions around shortening.} In a post hoc analysis aligned on the step at which each early-short run first fell below 100 tokens, the median lower clip fraction rose from 0.07\% of response tokens 11--15 steps earlier to 0.49\% in the preceding five steps and 0.91\% in the following five, while the upper clip fraction stayed at 0.03--0.08\%. In steps 1--5, early-short and other runs did not differ (median 0.015\% each), so this diagnostic accompanied shortening rather than anticipating it. Early-short runs then ended in either entropy regime, from 0.06 nats to more than 5 nats at step 300.

\noindent\textbf{KL loss in resumed runs.} The seven explosion runs were resumed from a checkpoint saved before entropy onset (steps 50--250) and trained to step 300 without KL, with $\beta=0.001$, and with $\beta=0.05$ (the 0.001 arm covers five runs), one run per arm. Onset is the first step after step 20 from which entropy stays above 1 nat for five steps. The resumed no-KL copies did not repeat their originals exactly: onset moved by $-35$ to $+24$ steps, and PPO seed 14 stayed below 1 nat. With $\beta=0.001$, entropy exploded in all five runs (PPO seed 14 only with KL), with onset $-4$ to $+31$ steps from the no-KL copies; the penalty was at most about 0.003, against a mean absolute policy loss of 0.007--0.12. With $\beta=0.05$, onset came 34--105 steps later in five runs, 27 steps earlier in DAPO-style seed 15, and only with KL in PPO seed 14; step-300 entropy was 0.63--3.20 nats, versus 5.14--9.44 for the six no-KL copies whose entropy exploded, and six of seven runs still ended above 1 nat. Responses also shortened, and step-300 Countdown accuracy was 2.8--29.7 points lower in the three group-relative runs.

\section*{Supplementary Note S4. Additional Coverage Details}
Supplementary Table S4 reports sampled pass@$k$ for every checkpoint. The base model solved 461 of the 500 MATH-500 items at least once in 64 samples. Apart from PPO seed 10, each checkpoint failed on 12--28 of these items in all 64 samples and solved 4--11 items the base model never solved, so the coverage loss is a net change rather than a uniform shift. Item-level counts are noisy, however: resampling the base model with four other seeds lost 7--12 and gained 3--8 of the same items. Sampling used a 4,096-token cap. The base model reached the cap on 2.3\% of MATH-500 samples, and the checkpoints that did not shorten early did so on 3.0--7.6\%; the early-short checkpoints reached it less often (1.2--4.6\%) yet lost similar coverage, so truncation alone does not account for the deficit. PPO seed 10 reached the cap on 78.5\% of MATH-500 and 71.7\% of GSM8K samples, consistent with the repetition seen under greedy decoding.
